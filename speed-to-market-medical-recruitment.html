Why speed to market matters in medical recruitment

The right doctor is often only available for a short window. The organisations that move quickly are usually the ones that get them.

Insights · The Doctor Network · October 2026 · 4 min read

In most industries, a slow hiring process is frustrating. In medicine, it can mean a roster gap that leaves patients waiting, a service relying on expensive locum cover for longer than it needs to, or a team stretched thin until someone burns out. And in specialties with real shortages, like psychiatry and general practice in many areas, a slow process often means losing the right doctor to someone faster.

The best doctors are rarely on the market for long

Most good doctors aren't actively job hunting. They become available briefly: between contracts, after a life change, or when something about their current role stops working. When that window opens, they usually have more than one option. The organisation that responds first, with clear information and a real conversation, has a big advantage.

Where time gets lost

  • Unclear briefs. If the role, rate or start date isn't settled, the search stalls before it starts.
  • Slow feedback. A CV that sits unread for a week often means a doctor who has accepted something else.
  • Scheduling interviews. Busy clinicians on both sides make this harder than it should be.
  • Credentialing. Collecting registration, references, checks and documents can add weeks if it starts late.
  • Disconnected systems. Information spread across emails, spreadsheets and databases slows everyone down.

How to move faster without cutting corners

Settle the essentials early. Agree on the rate, dates and scope before going to market. It's the single biggest time-saver.

Commit to quick feedback. Even "not for us" within a day or two keeps the process moving and protects your reputation with candidates.

Start credentialing in parallel. Begin collecting documents as soon as there's genuine mutual interest, rather than after an offer.

Keep warm relationships. The fastest searches start with doctors you already know. That's why we focus on staying connected with doctors long before they're looking.

Speed with care

Moving quickly doesn't mean skipping steps. Registration, references and credentialing matter, and they should never be rushed through. The aim is to remove waiting time, not checks.
