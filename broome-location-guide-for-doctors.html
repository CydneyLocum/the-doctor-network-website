Location Spotlight: Broome

Pearling history, Cable Beach sunsets and the gateway to the Kimberley. Here's what doctors should know about working in Broome.

Insights · The Doctor Network · October 2026 · 5 min read

Broome sits on the far north-west coast of Western Australia, roughly two and a half hours' flight from Perth. It's a small town with a big reputation: turquoise water, red pindan cliffs and a mix of cultures shaped by more than a century of pearling. For doctors, it's also one of the most rewarding places to work in regional Australia.

The health services

Broome Hospital is the regional hospital for the Kimberley, run by WA Country Health Service. It supports a network of smaller hospitals and clinics spread across a huge area, so doctors in Broome often see a broader range of presentations than they would in a metropolitan service.

For psychiatry, the Kimberley Mental Health and Drug Service is based in Broome. The hospital campus includes Mabu Liyan, a 13-bed acute mental health unit whose name means "a place of good spirit". It serves the Kimberley and Pilbara, and works closely with a dedicated Aboriginal mental health team.

The work

Around half of the Kimberley's population identifies as Aboriginal, and culturally safe care is central to the work. Doctors who do well here are curious, flexible and genuinely interested in working with Aboriginal communities, liaison officers and health workers. Many describe it as the most meaningful work of their career.

Expect variety. Distances are large, outreach is common, and you'll often be working with less specialist backup on hand than in a city. That brings real autonomy, and it's worth being honest with yourself about how comfortable you are with it.

The climate

Broome has two seasons. The dry season, roughly May to October, brings clear skies, low humidity and days around 18 to 30 degrees. It's also peak tourist season, so accommodation is tighter. The wet season, roughly November to April, is hot and humid with heavy storms, and some roads can flood during the monsoon months. If you're considering a locum, the season makes a real difference to the experience.

Life outside work

  • Cable Beach for sunset swims, walks and the famous camel rides.
  • Staircase to the Moon, the full-moon reflection over Roebuck Bay, usually between April and October.
  • Whale watching from around June to September, and dolphin cruises on Roebuck Bay.
  • The Kimberley on your doorstep, including gorges, waterfalls and some of the most remote landscapes in Australia.
  • Fishing, crabbing and boating, plus a strong local food and arts scene.

Is Broome right for you?

Broome suits doctors who want meaningful work, a slower pace outside the hospital and a genuine sense of adventure. It's less suited to anyone who needs to be close to family in a capital city or who wants a lot of specialist backup on site.

Locum roles are a great way to try it out first. Regional locum packages often include travel and accommodation, though every role is different.
